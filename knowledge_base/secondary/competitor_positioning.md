# Competitor Positioning

Secondary research layer. How other AI consultants and firms currently position themselves, used to identify gaps and avoid sounding like everyone else.

## The common patterns in the market

Most AI consulting positioning falls into one of two camps:

**Full-stack technical firms.** Position on capability: "strategy to implementation," "data to AI to integration to MLOps," proprietary platforms, large consultant headcounts. Language leans on scale and delivery capacity ("1,200+ consultants worldwide," "mid to low seven figures for full-cycle delivery"). Little differentiation between firms beyond client logos and industry lists.

**People-first / adoption-focused consultancies.** A smaller but growing group explicitly reframes AI adoption as a people and change-management problem, not a technology problem. One UK consultancy's framing: most organizations spend the bulk of their budget on technology while adoption actually fails on trust, unclear use cases, and weak change management. This group is closer to where I sit, but most of them stop at "adoption is a people problem" as a slogan, without a named, reusable framework for diagnosing why.

## The gap

Almost nobody in this space leads with a named, documented failure-pattern framework backed by a published book. Most positioning is capability-first ("we do strategy, we do implementation") or values-first ("AI adoption is a people problem"), but not diagnosis-first. Nobody's showing up with "here are the six specific ways this fails, and I can tell you which one you're in before we start building anything."

## Where I differ

- Framework-led, not capability-led. The six failure patterns are the product, not the delivery team.
- Diagnosis before build. Most competitors sell strategy or sell implementation. I sell figuring out if the use case is sound before either happens.
- Solo practitioner with a design background, not a firm with headcount to justify. That's a constraint most competitors don't have to work around, and it's also the credibility source: I've done the actual product design work, not just advised on it.
- Semi-technical positioning is rare. Most firms are either pure strategy (removed from implementation reality) or pure technical delivery. Sitting in the middle, credible with both engineers and executives, is an underused position.

## Why This Matters for Content and Consulting Work

Content should lean into the diagnosis-first, framework-led angle, since that's the actual white space. Avoid language that sounds like the capability-first firms ("full-stack," "end-to-end delivery," "scalable AI systems") since that's not the differentiator and it's not true positioning. Avoid vague "AI adoption is a people problem" framing too, since that's already crowded. Lead with the named framework and the diagnosis.
