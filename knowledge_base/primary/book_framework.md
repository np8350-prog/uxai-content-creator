# The Interface Is Not the System

Core thesis: AI adoption failures come from treating the visible interface as the whole product. Most teams design and ship the screen. They skip the system underneath it. That gap is where projects break.

The book documents six failure patterns, based on real client and industry work.

## The Six Failure Patterns

### 1. Solving the Wrong Problem
Teams build AI features because AI is available, not because a real problem needs it. The feature works. It just doesn't matter. Nobody asked for it, and nobody's workflow gets better because of it.

### 2. Waiting for Perfect Data
Teams delay shipping until the data is "clean enough." Data is never clean enough. This becomes an excuse to avoid the harder work: talking to users, defining scope, making a decision.

### 3. Forgetting the Humans
AI features get rolled out without user research or warning. I've seen this firsthand: employees hit by a sudden capability change with no context, no heads up, no chance to adjust. The result is shock and distrust, even when the feature itself works fine.

### 4. Underestimating Total Cost of Ownership
The demo costs almost nothing. Running it in production, monitoring it, fixing it when it drifts, that's the real cost. Teams budget for the build. They don't budget for what comes after.

### 5. AI Literacy Gaps at the Top
Executives often treat AI adoption as excitement, not strategy. I've watched leadership teams chase the feeling of "we're doing AI" instead of asking what problem it solves and how they'll know it worked. Excitement is not a plan.

### 6. No Clear Path Beyond Proof of Concept
A POC gets built, it gets a good demo reaction, and then it stalls. No one defined what happens after the POC succeeds. Without that path, most AI projects die in this exact spot.

## Why This Matters for Content and Consulting Work

Any content built from this framework should name a specific pattern, not talk about "AI challenges" in general. Specificity is the point. Generic AI commentary is exactly the kind of output this framework argues against.

When writing content that references this book, pull the actual pattern name and a concrete detail (a symptom, a moment, a consequence) rather than restating the six patterns as a list. The list is reference material, not the content itself.
